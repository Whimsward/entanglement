| David |
|-----|

“David?” Lila asks. Her eyes are narrow when she asks, and though her lips move the sound of her voice is muffled.

There’s a smell in the air: hot metal and burning plastic. Smoke creeps out the garage door. Out of the corner of his eye David sees the mingled branches of the trees in the front yard.  None of this can pull his attention off of his sister.

“Are you okay?” He crosses the short driveway to reach her.

Her face hardly moves until she strains out a chuckle. “That was my question.”

Still closing in, he says, “No. Li. You look like hell.”

“Which circle?”

“The cold one.”

“You look all right,” Lila observes. “Smell a bit like soot.”

David looks at Lila’s car in the garage. The door’s been torn up like a dissected ribcage. The smoke he spotted earlier is pouring out from the front seat, but he can’t see any flames. “I don’t think it’s me.”

She doesn’t look away from him. In fact, her eyes aren’t moving at all. Her body stiff, she says, “You need to leave before he comes back.”

“Leave?” David starts to reach out. Something stops him. “If I’ve got it right he won’t be back while I’m here. They mangled the spell to banish him and made us switch places instead. Apparently it didn’t stick.”

Lila makes her head nod. “So you’ve been… somewhere else.”

“Yeah.”

There should be more damage. The way that Mere and Mustang would talk about Thrast, he’d imagined that there would be carnage back on Earth. There’s just the car. And Lila. He meets her eyes. With as still as they are it’s… hard to do. “What did he do? You can tell me.”

Lila withers from the question, but it happens on a delay. She asks, “Do you think he’ll be back?”

“I don’t see why not, Li. It’s happened twice, without warning and for no reason I can see. Whatever they did must have stamped us on two sides of a coin and sent it spinning.”

“He says he wants to fix Earth.”

“He what?”

Lila’s lips get lazy again. Maybe she hopes David won’t notice, but her voice starts before her mouth moves. “He asked me what was wrong with this world, and showed me a shattered planet, and said he’d stop at nothing to prevent the ruin he foresaw.”

David shivers, there’s no emotion he feels with it. It’s a reaction his body has to some buried thought triggered by what Lila says. “World-ending monsters with messiah complexes. The best.” He pauses, trying to give his sister a bit longer to answer his actual question. Then, “I don’t think he can be hurt.”

“No,” Lila agrees. “He’s a lich.”

“And people on the other side are afraid to give their names because of what it might let him to do them.”

“He can reach into your mind and take over your body,” Lila says.

David’s stomach drops. He sees those soldiers, raised from their graves and glaring at his soul. His alarm simmers into a cold rage. He asks, “Why did he let you go?”

“I think he didn’t want to kill me to learn what I know.” The air around her jacket swims like a mirage. “His mistake.”

"Li.”

The look on her stiffened face is unfamiliar. He’s seen her angry, and this is not how it looks. Usually her eyes turn to ice. Now, though, her eyes are as almost-vacant as they’ve been since he returned. But he can feel the shift in the atmosphere that always came with her rage, and this time it is not just a series of unconscious signals. It is a wave of heat that blisters the air.

She stops hiding her hand.

The flesh is burned almost to the bone, and the ring she has worn since seventh grade rages white-hot. The air around the ring steams.

David’s hands are frozen, half-stretched towards his sister. She is not the one who stopped them this time. He asks, “What happened to you?”

“I’m working on it.”

“That’s not an answer.”

“I don’t have a good one.”

David’s voice strains. “Try?”

“Thrast brought magic here. When he used his magic on me, I woke to it. I’m still figuring it out, but--”

“Look at your hand!”

“I know.”

“I knew I was going to be too late.”

“David. There is a man with unimaginable power running amok on two worlds, and *<u>his philosophy is bad</u>*. We have to do something about it.”

“No, *I* have to do something about it. I’m the one tied up in this. You could have just backed away and stayed safe.”

“You know,” Lila says with cold eyes and a voice matching the rising temperature in the air, “that I would never back away from something like this.”

“Yeah I do know. Now look at you.”

“And what are you going to do?”

David takes out his phone. His hands are shaking. “I don’t even get to look him in the eyes.” He swipes to his contacts, taps a name. “Tell me he doesn’t look like me.”

“Fortunately for him.” Lila’s voice is flat.

His mom picks up. “Hi honey.”

“Mom,” David says. “Don’t come home.” He locks eyes with Lila.

“Sorry, I think we’re going through some bad coverage. What was that?”

His view of Lila starts to mist over. “Don’t come home. Don’t led Dad come home. Get a hotel and wait. Don’t look for me. Don’t look for Lila.”

“David Huxley, I’m not paying for your phone so you can--”

“Not joking, Mom.” His voice breaks.  “It’s bad. The house isn’t safe. I’ll be in touch. Just don’t look for us.”

“You don’t get to take that tone with me,” Jenna Adamson says over the phone. “Stop scaring your mother and tell me what’s going on right now.”

“There’s a… dangerous criminal on the loose. He’s taking hostages.” He wants to look anywhere but at his sister. He doesn’t look away.

Jenna says, “Oh my God what.”

“I love you.”

David hangs up.

The air has gone from a dry hot to a humidity that should make David sweat. It doesn’t. “You know that didn’t work,” Lila says.

David turns towards the road. “Yeah. Let’s get the hell away from the house.”
