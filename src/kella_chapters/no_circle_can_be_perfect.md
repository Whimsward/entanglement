# No Circle Can Be Perfect

| Kella |
|-----|

After Mere leaves her, Kella turns her attentions to Veneth at large. Her first curiosity, and simplest task, is to tenderly probe a world no longer held by the throat.

Ea’river’s cities have not noticed Thrast’s absence. What few servants Thrast retains who are Aligned with a Paradigm are in some ways emptier husks than most of those who toil in his directive to “elevate” the peoples of Veneth to his standards. They go about their appointed tasks with the diligence of beasts of burden, and even in his absence Thrast’s influence on them is too strong for Kella to find purchase without overextending herself. And she is, as she must be, very careful about how far she reaches.

The Gentlefishers sail turbulent seas, untouched by the storms, and carry food and material between the nations as ever they have. Kella passes them by. There is nothing to gain from them, or their approach to life.

Qastra is barren, haunted only by the remnants of the Legion and its grounds. Here, too, Kella must be careful, because what life remains in Qastra is wholly consumed by Thrast’s intent. *Except*, as Kella finds in her brief survey, there is an unaligned fishing village that has appeared on a Qastran shoreline, braving the possibility of detection.

The Far Halls of Kolomaer, across the sea, thrive with Anshall on his seat. Its people have thus far evaded Thrast’s Domination, in no small part because of the many trade agreements, brokered peaces, and relative harmlessness of the kingdom. Thrast’s attentions, however, were beginning to turn there before he vanished, so Anshall’s anxiety radiates out into his subjects, fostering in them a growing dread.

Goletha is empty, no longer even haunted by those who once lived within. It has been generations since Goletha’s impenetrable fortresses were cleared of their occupants. Where did Thrast send them? How did he take their permanence upon himself? Questions to which Kella does not have the time or talent to find the answers.

While Kella feels her way through Veneth, the queen travels other paths, probing a network of realms that Kella hesitates to observe even at distance. Mere will find Lenn out there in the winds of reality, but it will take time. During that time, what can Kella do but watch and wait? Well, one thing.

ella reaches out to Anshall. He is in anguish, even after the relief of the Circle’s rebinding. The closest members of his court suffer with him, not knowing the reason for his turmoil but expressing silent sympathy nonetheless. There are business transactions and other matters of state to sign off on, but his aides delegate most of these away, and the most pressing are delayed, without being afforded a chance to burden Anshall’s mind. It is the way of the Far Halls, a sign of affection for the face of Kolomaer. It is a small comfort, while Anshall waits to die. But Thrast does not descend, and in time he notices Kella Aspenwood’s touch.

Once he becomes aware of their contact, she asks, *Are you still out of reach, Anshall?*

He murmurs too softly for the members of his court to hear. “Kella, I am as distant from you as I will ever be.”

On impulse, as ever, she sends a surge of the deepest warmth to him, her unspoken secret exposed as a tender-flower-beating-heart gift for him. He breathes it in, as he always does, but neither Anshall nor Kella can take the risk to join in truth. Neither can he reciprocate, except to increase what love she already bears him.

She feels that love, not his but a gift from him nonetheless, and fills herself with it. It may seem a false hope offered as a consolation, but Kella has delved through its layers on many lonely nights: he is a physical man, and she is a distant presence; both of their Alignments are necessary to sustain the Circle, so coming together is too great a risk to the whole; if Lenn and Persaval must remain apart for the sake of all Veneth and the structure of reality, then even a mutual pining seems an act of disrespect. So he stokes her love, for which she longs but also on which she thrives.

Kella tells him they have failed. There is a boy who has taken Thrast’s place on Veneth, and the boy’s home is doomed to suffer as all Veneth has under the wizard’s scrutiny. But, and she offers this as a plea to Anshall, there is a thread of hope. A game of questions Lost Lenn played with an angel.

“You would have me pull on that thread.”

*Weave it as you will, my distant sovereign.*

On Veneth, where belief holds sway over reality, it is difficult for even a master Aligned with Edification to foster the growth of a concept, especially one as tenuous as hope. But he begins to dig into it, seizing on the bonds of the Circle and guiding Kella’s far-reaching mind towards the knowledge he needs to lift hope high. It lifts his head to the surface of the water, and sustains him for the agonizing time he must spend waiting to be visited by the Queen-errant.

Having shared what she can, she withdraws from him.

While Thrast is gone, Veneth quivers. Only after spreading herself out over the realm does she really feel it. Thrast has burrowed his way into the bones of the world and anchored himself there. Those anchors hold despite the curse. There is another, somewhere hidden from Kella by Persaval’s Separation, who is bound to those anchors. She wonders again what will happen if those anchors are ripped free and there is nothing to take their place? How will Veneth move, when it is freed from Thrast?

Wondering this over the course of hours and the passing of a night on her mountain makes her increasingly anxious. Where is this boy through whom their hopes are threaded? Can she find him, despite the Sworn Sword?

Then Mere returns from Kolomaer and echoes Kella’s curiosity with a frustrated observation, *He wandered off with the boy*. When Mere returns, and utters this, Kella feels Veneth stop quivering. She swallows her fear and assures the queen that she will search, but now she is not sure she wants to find Persaval.

She starts from where Mere is now. A field, less than a day’s travel from where Kella thought she found the fishing village on the shores of Qastra. When Mere and Persaval used the Bridge to escape from Thrast’s tower, they must have come out there. Perhaps she could predict where, but Persaval hides himself with Separation and even blocks out her ability to detect certain things around him. This is why she did not know where the three went after departing Thrast’s tower, and it is why she could not find him until now.

But the village she thought she had found on the Qastran shores is gone. Or, when she tries to look for it, she cannot feel it. She warns Mere that something might be wrong, but Mere is not deterred.

Slowly, achingly, Veneth begins to hum, and as it does Kella Aspenwood retreats. Fear is a motivator, yes, but also wisdom. The peerless will of the grand wizard Thrast is clawing its way free from Persaval’s cage of Separation, and the bonds of the Circle must not be caught in his grip.
